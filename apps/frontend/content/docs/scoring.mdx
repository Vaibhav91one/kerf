Everything below is a description of `packages/shared/src/points.ts` — that file is the real source
of truth; this page is a snapshot of it, not a separate spec that could drift.

## Three terms, per session

A qualifying session (see the floor below) earns points from three terms, each rewarding a
different thing:

| Term | What it rewards |
|---|---|
| **Landed** | Edits that stuck, on a log scale — a 200-edit session can't bury a careful 20-edit one. |
| **Precision** | The share of edits that landed first try. The dominant term, on purpose. |
| **Focus** | Net edits landed per turn, capped at 3/turn — one giant write hits the ceiling instead of running away with the score. |

```
netEdits  = edits - editsRework
landed    = round(10 * log2(1 + netEdits))
precision = round(30 * (1 - reworkRatio))
focus     = round(20 * min(1, (netEdits / turns) / 3))

sessionPoints = min(120, landed + precision + focus)
```

A session that doesn't qualify — fewer than 3 human turns, or zero edits that stuck — scores `0`
outright. It still uploads (so the CLI's "N sessions parsed" count is honest), it just contributes
nothing.

## Totals and caps

Points sum across sessions, but each UTC calendar day is capped at 300 points before it's added to
your total. Two numbers matter:

- **Lifetime total** → your **level** (Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum → Diamond), which never
  resets.
- **This month's total** → your rank on the [Season](/season) board, which resets every UTC
  calendar month.

They're genuinely different numbers on purpose — a big lifetime total can sit at Gold while this
month's total is small, and that's not a bug, it's two different questions being answered.

## Why points instead of a raw ratio

The scoring engine underneath all of this is still the "rework ratio" idea — the share of edits that
had to be redone. Earlier, the app showed that ratio directly (`0.369`) next to a percentile cutoff
(`p20 cut is 0.31`) — accurate, but not readable to a visitor. Points and fixed-threshold levels are
the same signal, translated into something you can look at for two seconds and understand.

## Farming it doesn't work

Any scoring system invites the question "how would someone game this," so the counters are built in
rather than bolted on:

1. **Non-qualifying sessions score zero.** Running the CLI more without doing real work doesn't
   help.
2. **Daily cap.** A bot loop plateaus at 300 points/day instead of compounding.
3. **Sublinear volume, dominant precision.** The award per session grows slower than the edit count,
   and the precision term is the biggest of the three — padding a session with volume barely moves
   it.

This is a deliberate, documented departure from the original design goal of "never rank on a
cumulative total" — taken because leveling is worth having, on the condition that it ships with
exactly these counters against the obvious way to abuse a total.

## The season qualification floor

Showing up on the monthly board at all requires **10 qualifying sessions and 5 commits** in the same
UTC month — a `git commit --allow-empty` loop clears the second half for free, but the floor only
gates *ranking eligibility*. It earns zero points on its own; the ten qualifying sessions still have
to be real, honestly-scored sessions.
